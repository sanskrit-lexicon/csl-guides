# How to read the PW (Böhtlingk's shorter dictionary)

Böhtlingk's *Sanskrit-Wörterbuch in kürzerer Fassung* (`PW`, seven parts, 1879–1889) is
the dictionary most readers of German actually keep at hand: the whole headword coverage
of the seven-volume [Großes Petersburger Wörterbuch](../dictionaries/pwg), corrected and
extended, at a fraction of the bulk. But "shorter" was achieved by a set of deliberate
conventions — an asterisk that flags dictionary-only words, citations that assume you can
reach the larger work, accents only where the sources have them. This page reads the
front matter so you don't have to learn those conventions by being burned.

For **online lookup mechanics** and the entry markup see the
[PW dictionary guide](../dictionaries/pw); the complete front matter, with English and
Russian translations, is in [OCR'd prefaces](../dictionaries/ocr-prefaces).

## What the Foreword declares

The single Foreword page of 1879
([English translation](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/PWK/blob/main/prefaces/pwpref02.en.md))
sets out the contract with the reader:

> The new dictionary was thus not meant to supplant the older one, but … to improve where
> something was to be improved, and to add where something was lacking, while, by the
> omission of all the citations and passages given there, constantly reminding the reader
> that the larger dictionary must remain the principal source.

The operative conventions, in the Foreword's own order:

- **The asterisk `*`** marks "a word, a meaning, a construction or a gender hitherto
  adduced only by grammarians or lexicographers" — the single most important reading mark
  in the book. No asterisk means the form is attested in literature; with it, you are
  looking at the Indian grammatical tradition's testimony only. (A grammarian *using* a
  word as an author, though, counts as literature — and the *Bhaṭṭikāvya*, as a versified
  Pāṇini exercise, is denied "the name of an independent literary work".)
- **Accents appear only for words that occur in accented (Vedic) texts.** Where only a
  vocative was attested, the accent is left undetermined and the source work is named
  instead.
- **Quoted passages are gone by design** — a PW entry gives you the judgment and the
  reference; the evidence lives in PWG. The two books are meant to be used as a pair.
- **The work was collaborative:** Roth, Kern, Stenzler and Weber are credited as the four
  main contributors of corrections and new material, with a dozen further scholars named.
- Spelling variants in व/ब and श/ष/स are listed **once**, under the older or better
  attested spelling — check the other spelling before concluding a word is absent.

## The sigla you will actually meet

The "Abkürzungen der Werke" legend (three front-matter pages) declares **302
abbreviations**; the digital body attests 209 of them (69%). As in PWG, the shortest
sigla mark the most-used sources. Counts are measured occurrences in the digital text
(`csl-orig/v02/pw/pw.txt`), from the site's legend feed
[`pref-legends.json`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-guides/blob/main/src/data/pref-legends.json):

Conventions the table already illustrates:

- **A superscript number is an edition switch:** `VP.²` (594 citations, not shown above)
  is the *second* edition of Wilson's Viṣṇupurāṇa translation, ed. Hall — a different
  book from `VP.`.
- **A parenthesised name is a channel, not a work:** `(Kern)`, `(Roth)`, `(Garbe und
  Roth)` in the legend record *who supplied or checked* the source.
- **Default edition unless marked:** `Çâk.` cites Böhtlingk's own Śākuntala edition
  unless `Pisch.` (Pischel) or `Premaḱ.` (Premacandra) is appended — the same
  edition-discipline as PWG's `R.`.
- Note the recension shift against PWG: the Ṛgveda siglum is spelled `ṚV.` here but `RV.`
  in PWG, and `MBh.` now points into the **Bombay** edition where PWG cited the Calcutta
  print. Sigla do not transfer between the two dictionaries unexamined.

The full legend with every key, expansion and count is rendered on the
[PW guide's abbreviations section](../dictionaries/pw#abbreviations).

## The grammatical layer

The legend itself is almost entirely work sigla (301 of 302; the one grammar-classed key
is `Pischel, de Gr. pr.` — Pischel's *De grammaticis prācriticis*, 25 citations). The
grammatical *authorities* are cited as works: `P.` (Pāṇini, 13,706), `Pat. zu P.`
(Patañjali on Pāṇini, 172), `Kâty. Śr.` and kin for the ritual sūtras. But PW's real
metalanguage lives **inside the entry**, not in the legend:

- the **asterisk** described above — the grammarian-only flag;
- the grammatical **tags** (`<ab>…</ab>` in the digital text: gender, *priv.*, case
  labels) — see the worked sample in the [PW guide](../dictionaries/pw#reading-an-entry);
- the **citation-free abridgement** itself: where PWG argues with the glossators at
  length, PW compresses the verdict into marks and word order.

So the literacy skill for PW is less "which grammarian is this?" (usually Pāṇini) and
more "is this form literature or lexicography?" — exactly what the asterisk answers.

## The rare keys are intentional

302 declared keys against 209 attested is, as with PWG, a typed and explainable gap, not
noise:

- **Manuscript sources carry real weight.** `Âpast. Śr.` — a manuscript, per the legend —
  has 1,718 body citations; `Bhâvapr.` combines a Calcutta print with a manuscript;
  `Maitr. S.` (1,507) was cited from L. Schröder's edition *while it was still being
  prepared for publication* — which is why some of its accents only appear in the
  addenda. The manuscript **catalogues** are sources too: `Verz. d. Oxf. H.` (Aufrecht's
  Oxford catalogue, 181 citations) and `Verz. d. B. H.` (Weber's Berlin catalogue, 67).
- **The measured residual is typed.** The census leaves 89 legend keys with no body match
  (pref-only); the feed carries a typed residual class on 26 keys: 16 `true_unused`
  (mostly external scholarship cited in the front matter, not in entries), 6 `ocr_key`
  (Böhtlingk's special letters `ḱ`, `ǵ` in keys like `Harshaḱ.`), 2 `ambiguous`, 1
  `rare`, 1 `ortho`. In the H1560 50-key sample the dominant residual cause was
  **orthography** — the legend's German *j* where the body writes *y* — a
  transliteration-era artifact, not a phantom source. Full decomposition:
  [pref-only rollup](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-guides/blob/main/scripts/out/PWG_PW_pref_only_decompose.md).

Meeting an unknown siglum in PW, assume first a spelling-variant of a declared key
(*j*/*y*, `ḱ`/`c`), then a front-matter-only reference — and remember the full evidence
trail for any entry continues in PWG.
