# Where MW's Supplement Came From

Monier-Williams' 1899 dictionary carries a **supplement** — the "annexure", entries tagged
`<info n="sup"/>` in the Cologne digitization of [`mw`](/dictionaries/mw). Where did those
entries come from? The obvious candidate is Böhtlingk's *Nachträge und Verbesserungen* (the
supplement sections of the great Petersburg dictionary, **PW 1855–1875**, digitized
separately as `pwkvn` and tagged `sup_1`…`sup_7` inside `pw.txt`).

That question is **measurable** rather than rhetorical, because the sources carry dates and
the Cologne digitizations are queryable.

## The enrichment metric

Take a headword set **S**. Compare its share among MW99's annexure entries with its share
among MW99's main entries:

> **enrichment(S) = [ |S ∩ annexure| / |annexure| ] ÷ [ |S ∩ main| / |main| ]**

A ratio ≫ 1 means the supplement is disproportionately stocked from S.

| source set S | n(S) | share in annexure | share in main | **enrichment** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| PW Nachträge, `sup_7` (letzte Nachträge) | 13,094 | 29.6 % | 3.8 % | **7.79×** |
| PW Nachträge, all layers `sup_1`…`sup_7` | 14,401 | 32.2 % | 4.1 % | **7.91×** |
| `pwkvn` standalone Nachträge digitization | 14,995 | 32.5 % | 4.1 % | **7.84×** |
| Edgerton, *Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit Dictionary* (1953) | 17,777 | 6.8 % | 3.3 % | **2.09×** |

Convention: `k1`-only headwords; MW99 annexure = 6,067 `k1`, main = 188,016 `k1`.

**Reading.** The 1899 annexure is **~7.8× enriched for PW-Nachträge vocabulary** and only
**~2.1× enriched for Edgerton's BHS vocabulary** — i.e. it is about **3.7× more
"PW-flavoured" than "BHS-flavoured"**. Edgerton (1953) post-dates MW99 by 54 years, so its
low enrichment is expected; the PW layers *pre-date* MW99 and their enrichment is high. The
asymmetry is the quantitative form of the long-standing suggestion that MW's supplement was
built on the Nachträge.

## The dates are the argument

| source | year | relation to MW99 (1899) |
|---|---|---|
| Böhtlingk–Roth, *Sanskrit-Wörterbuch* (PW), vols 1–7 + Nachträge | 1855–1875 | **pre-dates** — available to MW |
| Böhtlingk, *kürzere Fassung* | 1879–1889 | **pre-dates** — available to MW |
| Monier-Williams, MW72 | 1872 | earlier edition, **no annexure** |
| Monier-Williams, MW99 | 1899 | the dictionary with the annexure |
| Edgerton, BHS Dictionary | 1953 | **post-dates** — not available to MW |

Because every Nachträge layer post-dates MW72 and pre-dates MW99, a Nachträge word present
in MW99 but **absent from MW72** could only have been taken from the Nachträge. Of the
13,094 unique `sup_7` headwords, **4,112 appear in no MW edition at all**, 27 were dropped
between the editions, and MW99 took **6,090 (≈60 %)** of the pool MW72 lacked — 1,474 of
them through the annexure.

## A worked example

`kāritra` (abstract noun "activity", = *ceṣṭita*) is **absent from `mw.txt` entirely** and
from MW72 (1872), while it stands in the Nachträge at **PW 7-331-d** (`pw` L216013,
`pwkvn` L16013), citing **Mahāvyutpatti 245, 844** (ed. 1849) and glossed by Edgerton's
BHS as a Buddhist-Hybrid term. `kārāpaka`, its alphabetical neighbour, *did* enter the MW99
annexure — the shape of a supplement that was compiled from the Nachträge and skipped some
of its entries.

## Caveats

- The metric ranks **provenance likelihood**, not citation: set overlaps do not prove that a
  given annexure entry was copied from a given Nachträge page. The reverse share — ~30 % of
  annexure headwords are verbatim `sup_7` entries — is the direct check.
- The candidate lists are a **worklist**, not verdicts: every proposed addition needs
  printed-scan verification before filing.
- Near-form string matches are review flags, not matches (`kāritra` fuzzy-hits `kārita` at
  0.92 — a different word).
