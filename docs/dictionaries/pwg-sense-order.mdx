# Does PWG order its senses chronologically?

The *Poona Dictionary* (PD) is built on the OED model and claims *historical principles* — that
its senses run from older to newer. In October 2026 that claim was finally measured
([csl-atlas PD sense-order report](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/reports/PD_SENSE_ORDER_2026.md)):
PD's senses do tend to rise in date, but only as a tendency (τ = +0.32 on the Dharmamitra clock,
a median drift of +265 years from first to last sense, and 40 % of two-sense entries running the
other way).

A tendency means nothing without a control. **Böhtlingk–Roth (PWG) is that control** — a
dictionary of the same century with no OED remit — and the answer it gives is the reason this
page exists: **PWG orders its senses *more* chronologically than PD does.** So PD's sense order
is not distinctive, and "historical principles" is not evidenced by the arrangement of senses.

:::note The measured object
This page is about the order of **senses inside an entry**, not the order of quotations inside a
sense. The latter is a different measurement, on a different unit — see the
[PD citation-order report](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/reports/PD_CITATION_ORDER_FULL_2026.md).
:::

## What was measured

For PWG, PW, MW and AP: each numbered sense division contributes the **earliest datable citation
filed under it**; the sequence of those dates in sense order is then tested for a rising
tendency. Two date instruments are used, and both were already the ones behind the PD figure, so
the comparison is like-for-like:

- **DCS** — the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit chronology, matched at Dice ≥ 0.90 to the work a
  citation abbreviation names.
- **Dharmamitra** — its post-median date for the work, earliest stratum of a base title, matched
  at Dice ≥ 0.82.

Citation abbreviations are resolved from each dictionary's own key
([csl-guides abbreviations](abbreviations-and-citations.mdx)), longest-prefix; AP
borrows the documented Apte (AP90) key, and MW resolves through the vendored
`mw-source-abbreviations.tsv`. Thresholds are fixed and were never relaxed per dictionary.

The statistic is the one PD's own report uses, imported rather than re-implemented: the
two-sense **forward rate** (later sense, later date) with an exact Clopper–Pearson 95 % interval,
**Kendall τ** between sense position and earliest date for entries with at least three datable
senses, a 5,000-replicate within-entry **permutation null**, and the median first→last drift.

## The verdict

The comparison rule was fixed before the control numbers were computed, and the vocabulary is
its own: the claim **stands** only if PD beats *every* scored dictionary by τ ≥
{data.rule.effectFloor.toFixed(2)} with a Holm-adjusted one-sided permutation p ≤
{data.rule.alpha.toFixed(2)}; otherwise it is **withdrawn**.

**Outcome: {data.verdict.status}.** PWG's τ of {data.dictionaries[0].tau.toFixed(2)} sits

not decide the verdict on its own. PW and AP show no chronological order at all. The reading that
PD's sense arrangement is historically distinctive is therefore withdrawn; the tendency is shared
with dictionaries that do not carry the OED's programme.

## A worked entry: <i>guru</i>

PWG numbers a sense (`1〉`) and then subdivides it with lettered sub-senses (`a〉`, `— b〉`). The
measurement takes the **numbered** sense as the unit — a lettered sub-sense folds into its
parent, so the parent's date is the earliest among all its sub-senses, exactly as a nested
division behaves in PD. Trimmed from `csl-orig/v02/pwg/pwg.txt`, record **L 22685** (vol. 2,
p. 767):

```
<L>22685<pc>2-0767<k1>guru<k2>guru/
<div n="1"> 1〉 adj. f. {#gurvI#}; compar. {#ga/rIyaMs#}
  <ls>P. 6,4,157</ls>. <ls>VOP. 7,56</ls>. …
<div n="2"> a〉 {%schwer%} (<ab>Gegens.</ab> {#laGu#}) …
  <ls>ṚV. 1,39,3</ls>. <ls>AV. 9,3,24</ls>. …
<div n="2">— b〉 {%gross, ausgedehnt%}
  (dem äussern Umfange nach), = {#mahant#} … <ls>PAÑCAT. II, 199</ls>. …
```

*(Trimmed and wrapped for display; the record's own line breaks are different.)*

| Sense | What it holds | Earliest dated citation (Dharmamitra) |
|---|---|---|
| `1〉` adj. — *heavy*, *great*, *long* (with sub-senses a–e) | Ṛgvedic, with the classical grammarians alongside | **−1125** (Ṛg-Veda) |
| `2〉` m. — *a venerable person; father, mother, elder relative* | classical | **−369** |
| `3〉` f. — *pregnant woman; a teacher's wife* | lexicographers only, no datable work | — (skipped) |

The entry rises as the tendency predicts, and it also shows the two honest limits: the third
sense has no dated citation and is **skipped, not imputed**, and the earliest citation is the
earliest *quotation filed under the sense*, not a date Böhtlingk recorded. (Both figures are from
the 19-10-2026 run; the sense-level dates are not stored as data on this site.)

## What this lets you say — and not say

- PWG's arrangement is **meaning-driven first and historical second**: 54.8 % of two-sense
  entries run forward, so almost half do not. Treat sense position as a weak prior on age, never
  as a date.
- The forced canonical order (literal → figurative → technical) is shared by every dictionary
  and is exactly what the control measures; it is reported, never corrected away.
- The resolution ceiling is the binding constraint: {(100 * data.dictionaries[0].lsResolved / data.dictionaries[0].lsInstances).toFixed(1)} %
  of PWG's {data.dictionaries[0].lsInstances.toLocaleString('en-US')} `<ls>` citations reached a
  dated work, because PWG quotes lexicographers, grammarians and medieval compendia that the two
  chronologies do not date.
- DCS and Dharmamitra are **one lineage, not two instruments** (ρ = 0.94 on 56 shared works), so
  the two clocks replicate a chronology rather than doubling the evidence.

## Reproduce

The measurement lives in csl-atlas, not here:

- [`scripts/pd_sense_order_compare.py`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/scripts/pd_sense_order_compare.py)
  (`--build`, `--check`, `--render`) and its package
  [`scripts/pd_compare/`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/tree/main/scripts/pd_compare) —
  the PD row is recomputed from the committed TSV and must reproduce the PD summary exactly
  before any control is read.
- [`data/pd/pd_sense_order_compare_summary.json`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/pd/pd_sense_order_compare_summary.json) —
  every number on this page, with its denominators.
- [Report](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/reports/PD_SENSE_ORDER_COMPARE_2026.md) ·
  [nine pinned forensic tests](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/tests/forensic/test_pd_sense_order_compare.py)
  that fail if the verdict flips silently.

This page renders `src/data/pwg-sense-order.json`, a projection of that summary generated by
[`scripts/build-pwg-sense-order.mjs`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-guides/blob/main/scripts/build-pwg-sense-order.mjs)
(`npm run build:pwg-sense-order`, `--check` to verify). The source SHA-256 is recorded in the JSON,
so a re-run against a drifted artefact fails instead of re-vendoring silently.
