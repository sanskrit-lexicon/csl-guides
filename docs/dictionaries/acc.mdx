# Aufrecht, *Catalogus Catalogorum* (`ACC`)

`ACC` is Theodor Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum* — not a word dictionary but the great
**bibliographical catalogue of Sanskrit works and authors**, indexing titles and names against
the manuscript catalogues in which they appear. A finding-aid for Sanskrit literature: the
title means *a catalogue of catalogues*, and expresses, in Aufrecht's own words, "the sources
from which the present work is" compiled.

It is among the largest datasets on CDSL — **just under 50,000 entries** — and one of the most
*independent*: **43.3 % of its headwords occur in no other digitized dictionary here**, the
natural result of indexing work-titles, commentators, and library collections rather than
vocabulary.

## When to use it

Reach for `ACC` to find **where a Sanskrit work or author is catalogued** — its subject field
and the manuscript catalogues that record it. Concretely:

1. **Identify a work** behind a title met in a colophon or citation — its subject, its
   commentator, where manuscripts are listed.
2. **Trace a commentator or author** across the literature that carries their name.
3. **Locate manuscript holdings** as they stood in the printed catalogues of Aufrecht's day.

It indexes literature, not vocabulary; for word meanings use **[Monier-Williams (MW)](mw)**.

## Reading an entry

Each entry is a **title or author** (`<k1>`) with a subject tag and abbreviated catalogue
references. The first entry (`csl-orig/v02/acc/acc.txt`):

```
<L>1<pc>1-001,1<k1>aMSadaSA<k2>aMSadaSA
{#aMSadaSA#}¦ jy. Rice 28.
```

An author entry looks different again (`csl-orig/v02/acc/acc.txt`, entry 1925):

```
<L>1925<pc>1-048,1<k1>AnandarAya<k2>AnandarAya
{#AnandarAya#}¦ patron of Samaraja (Damacarita). Oxf. 138^b.
```

| In the source | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `<pc>1-048,1` | page-column reference (volume 1, p. 48, col. 1) |
| `{#aMSadaSA#}` | the **work title** *Aṃśadaśā* (search key `<k1>`, SLP1 in bold `{#…#}`) |
| `jy.` | subject abbreviation — *jyotiṣa* (astronomy/astrology) |
| `Rice 28` / `Oxf. 138^b` | the catalogue reference — which printed catalogue lists it, at what page or folio (`^b` = the second side of folio 138) |
| `patron of Samaraja (Damacarita)` | for authors: the relationship that identifies them |

See [Data Formats](../developers/data-formats) for the markup reference.

## The two abbreviation systems

Every entry compresses two facts into sigla, both keyed in the work's own front matter —
which survives on CDSL as a fully transcribed [digitized preface](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-doc/blob/master/source/dictionaries/prefaces/accpref.rst),
unlike most scan-only front matter here.

**Subject tags** are Aufrecht's terse field markers. His preface names them directly:
"*an.* anonymous, *dh.* dharma, *fr.* fragmentary, *gr.* grammatical, *ny.* nyaya,
*tantr.* tantric", alongside *jy.* (*jyotiṣa*) and others. Counted across the whole file, the
most common are `dh.` (dharma, ~1,950 entries), `jy.` (~1,580), `ny.` (~940), and `gr.` (~540)
— a fair map of what nineteenth-century libraries collected.

**Catalogue references** point into the printed manuscript catalogues the entries were
harvested from. From the same preface: `Oxf.` is the Bodleian catalogue Aufrecht himself wrote
(1864); `Cambr.` his Trinity College Cambridge catalogue (1869); `IO.` the India Office
collections (Colebrooke, Wilkins, Burnell and others, catalogued by Eggeling); `W.` Weber's
Berlin *Verzeichniss* (1853); `L.` Rajendralala Mitra's *Notices of Sanskrit MSS.* — plus
dozens of regional lists (a typical entry cites `Rice 28`). When you expand a reference like
`Oxf. 138^b` you get a shelf, not a definition: ACC tells you **where manuscripts were**, and
the reader follows from there.

## How the file is organized

The three parts were each alphabetized separately — the digitized user notes are explicit that
"the entries in each volume are separately alphabetized" — so the digital text runs through the
alphabet once per volume-part. The file also preserves the catalogue's internal structure as
section headings: clusters of related works sit under head-texts ("Commentaries on the
*Tarkasaṃgraha*", commentaries grouped two-by-two under their base works) and under named
anthology-collections (treatises "mostly extracted from the *Gadādharī*", tracts from the
*Jagadīśī*). Searching a base text's name can therefore surface a whole family of commentary
entries that no single alphabetical position would reveal.

## Search tips

1. **Titles, not meanings.** Search the Sanskrit title in SLP1 transliteration; there is no
   English gloss to search for.
2. **Mind the per-volume alphabets.** A title has up to three separate positions (Parts I, II,
   III); check all runs before concluding absence.
3. **Search commentators by name.** Author entries carry relationships ("patron of…",
   "commentator on…") that identify minor figures nowhere else.
4. **Follow section headers.** Commentary families cluster under their base work's heading.

## What makes it distinctive

- **A catalogue of catalogues.** The standard index of Sanskrit works and authors.
- **Bibliographical, not lexical** — it points to manuscripts and catalogues.
- **Fully transcribed front matter** — the preface with both abbreviation keys is searchable
  text on CDSL.
- **Highly independent headword stock** — 43.3 % unique share per the site's atlas
  measurements, see [Guides Hypotheses](../about/guides-hypotheses).

## Headword key conventions

`<k1>` = `<k2>` = SLP1 (Sanskrit title or author name as the search key). ACC is a **bibliographic catalogue**, not a word dictionary — entries identify works and their locations in manuscript catalogues.

| Convention | Marker / form | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search key | SLP1 (title / name) | `aMSadaSA` | Type the Sanskrit title or author name in SLP1 |
| Title form | SLP1 bold `{#…#}` | `{#aMSadaSA#}` | The printed title |
| Subject code | Short abbreviation after headword | `jy.` = *jyotiṣa* (astrology) | Subject field of the work |
| Catalogue reference | Catalogue name + page/folio | `Rice 28`, `Oxf. 138^b` | The manuscript catalogue that records the work |
| No English gloss | No definition | — | ACC gives location information, not meaning |
